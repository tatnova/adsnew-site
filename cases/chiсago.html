Meta Ads Case Study: Beauty Salon in Chicago, Illinois

Results: 794 inquiries, ~160 new clients in 2.5 months (Facebook + Instagram).

Project snapshot

  • Location: Chicago, Illinois, USA
  • Business type: Beauty salon
  • Channels: Facebook + Instagram (Meta Ads)
  • Daily ad budget: ~$100/day
  • Timeframe: 2.5 months
  • Outcome: the salon became profitable within the first month

Starting point

This beauty salon in Chicago had been operating since spring 2025. They already had a website and well-designed social media, but there were no stable bookings. Inquiries happened, yet they did not convert into appointments, and the business was gradually going into the red.

Before me, two ad specialists worked on the account. I became the third one at a stressful moment: staff started leaving and the owner felt “ads don’t work.”

Why previous ad launches didn’t work

After reviewing the past campaigns, I found the typical issue: there was no consistent strategy. Ads were launched without deep audience work, without structured testing of services, and without understanding which offers the local market actually needed. The user journey from click to booking was broken.

What I did before launch

In the 1–1.5 weeks before going live, I rebuilt the foundation:

  • audited previous campaigns and results
  • researched the salon’s target audience in Chicago
  • proposed and aligned several promotion strategies with the owner
  • created offers for different service categories
  • prepared ad creatives (photo + video)
  • checked and fine-tuned the ad account setup
  • prepared the base for response scripts to convert inquiries into bookings

Strategy: test multiple services, not just one

We ran ads on Instagram and Facebook, sending traffic to both social profiles and the website. Instead of betting on a single service, we tested multiple directions:

  • hair services
  • cosmetology / facial treatments
  • nail services
  • permanent makeup

We also tested different creative formats: professional photo/video, authentic “real-life” content, voiceover vs no voiceover, multiple hooks, messages, and offers. In beauty, creatives fatigue quickly — consistent results require ongoing updates.

Results

With a total ad budget of $100/day, the salon reached profitability within the first month — stable bookings started coming in, with a consistent flow of new clients.

  • 794 inquiries in 2.5 months
  • ~160 new clients
  • Average cost per acquired client: ~$45

Why LTV matters more than “first visit profit”

The salon’s average service price was around $150. Even if you only count the first visit, the acquisition cost made sense. But the real economics in beauty comes from repeat visits.

Clients come back 1–2 times a year (often 3–4). That puts estimated customer lifetime value (LTV) at roughly $300–$600+. With acquisition around $45, advertising becomes profitable not only short-term, but long-term.

What didn’t work (and why it’s normal)

Not every service performed immediately. Some directions were paused, others were scaled. Interestingly, a few services that didn’t work early started producing results later — once we learned which meanings and creatives the audience needed. This is normal for salon marketing: you don’t launch once and “leave it forever.”

Common mistakes salon owners make

  • starting advertising only after opening, instead of building demand 2+ weeks earlier
  • relying on one service instead of testing multiple entry points
  • expecting “fast results” in 1–2 weeks
  • no system: no testing plan, no conversion scripts, no creative rotation

Practical recommendations (US/Canada market)

  • Start ads at least 2 weeks before an opening or a big promotion.
  • Plan marketing for at least 3 months, not 7–10 days.
  • Test multiple services and offers to find the strongest “entry point.”
  • Keep your profiles clean and clear — in beauty, trust is part of conversion.
  • Rotate creatives regularly to avoid fatigue.
